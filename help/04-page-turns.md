# Page turns

A sentence split across a page turn makes the reader stop mid-sentence to turn the page. When RT Press builds your book, it can start that sentence on the next page instead.

## The three choices

- **Mend, adding no pages**: the usual choice. A sentence is moved only where its chapter has room, so the page count stays as it was.
- **Mend, pages may be added**: more turns can be mended, and the book may grow. If you have pinned the page count, it grows only as far as that count allows.
- **Leave as set**: nothing is moved.

The build says how many page turns split a sentence before the repair and after it. Only a page whose text carries on is counted: the last page of a chapter can't split a sentence. Some turns can't be mended, such as one in a chapter's first paragraph.

## The page turn report

After a build, open **Page turn report**, under the build's summary. It lists:

- **Still split**: each page turn that still splits a sentence, and why it was left.
- **Left split on purpose**: the ones on your list.
- **Moved to the next page**: every sentence the repair moved, with the page it now starts.
- Any sentence on your list that is at no page turn now, and the pages that end above or below the bottom line.

Choose a page number to see that page.

## Keeping a turn split

A split can be worth keeping, when turning the page adds to the suspense. Choose **Keep split** beside the sentence, then build again. **Mend it again** takes it off your list.

The list belongs to your book and is saved with it. It goes by the sentence's words, so it still works after you bring the chapters in again. If you change the sentence, add it again. A sentence that appears more than once is left split wherever it falls on a page turn.

The repair never changes your words. It only decides which page a sentence starts on. A page that ends early is the cost of moving a sentence off it.

When a sentence moves, whatever was before it on that line stays behind. That line ends short, as the last line of a paragraph does. Only a line that is nearly full already is spread to the full width, like the lines above it.

With *Mend, adding no pages*, a chapter can run onto the blank page that followed it. Every chapter still starts where it did, and the book has the same number of pages.

Repairing takes longer than setting the book: for a short novel, anything from a few seconds to two minutes.

## Words stay whole

No page that carries on ends in the middle of a word. Where one would, RT Press keeps that word whole and sets the book again, as long as that moves no chapter and splits no more sentences. If it can't, the checks say which page.

## Even page bottoms

A page that ends early leaves the facing pages uneven. With **Even page bottoms** on, every page whose text carries on overleaf is brought down to the same last line, where that can be done gently. The spare space is shared out: a little more between the lines, and before the paragraphs on that page. No word moves to another page.

Some pages are left as they are, because they would need too much space in one place, such as a chapter's first page with its large first letter, or a page filled by one long paragraph. A page with a scene break can end slightly lower than the rest. The build says how many pages end above or below the bottom line.
